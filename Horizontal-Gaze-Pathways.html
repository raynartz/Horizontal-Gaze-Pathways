Interpretation

Teaching points

A horizontal saccadic command activates the PPRF on the side toward which the patient looks. The ipsilateral abducens nucleus supplies the ipsilateral lateral rectus through CN VI. Its internuclear neurons cross and ascend in the opposite MLF to the medial rectus subnucleus of CN III.

A peripheral CN VI lesion impairs ipsilateral abduction. An abducens nucleus lesion impairs conjugate gaze toward that side because both its motor and internuclear outputs are interrupted. The PPRF selection illustrates impaired ipsilateral horizontal saccadic commands; other eye movement systems are outside this simulation.

An MLF lesion impairs ipsilateral adduction on gaze away from the lesion, with possible abducting nystagmus in the opposite eye. One-and-a-half syndrome combines an ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy with ipsilateral INO. The remaining horizontal movement is contralateral abduction.

Model limitations

This simulator shows complete pathway interruptions and schematic movement amplitudes. It starts from neutral alignment to emphasize movement deficits; resting esotropia, exotropia, and other deviations are not modeled. Clinical INO may instead show slowed or partially restricted adduction. Nystagmus timing and amplitude are illustrative.

The animated transitions are slowed for teaching and are not physiological saccade waveforms. Convergence, pursuit, VOR, vertical gaze, ptosis, and pupil responses are not simulated. The CN III checkbox represents only its medial rectus pathway, not the full phenotype of CN III palsy. A CN VI nerve lesion plus an MLF lesion should not be equated with an abducens nucleus lesion.
